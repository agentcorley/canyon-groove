Where this came from. When I saw this photograph, where the magnified microscopic record player needle sits between the vinyl record’s tracks I thought the groove walls looked like canyon walls. They look ridged and layered, and the shape of those walls is the music. I wondered whether it works the other way round. Could you take a real canyon, treat the cross-section as a groove, and use AI to drag a needle through the canyon grooves?

How it plays. Each canyon is a real elevation transect, rim to rim, 100 samples. The floor elevation picks the key. The canyon’s symmetry picks the mood: balanced walls play bright, lopsided ones play dark. Every voice has its own stylus crossing the profile at its own speed, and the speeds never divide evenly, so the voices drift in and out of phase the way Brian Eno’s tape loops did. Notes ring when a stylus crosses a contour line, so steep walls chime and flat floors fall silent. Echo scales with depth. Deeper canyon, longer tail.

Make it yours. Every voice has a handful of instruments and its own level, attack, decay, tone, grit, echo, and delay. The URL carries all of it, plus the canyon, key, mood, and seed. Copy the link and someone else hears the same song. Export two minutes and you get an MP3.
